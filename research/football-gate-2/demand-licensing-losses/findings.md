# Football creator demand, licensing affordability and loss evidence

2026-09-19 · Codex · Branch `research/football-gate-2-demand-licensing-losses`, based on pending-evidence branch commit `2e2601d`. Twelve original targets: eleven readable, one blocked. Earlier research is preserved.

## Result

**Demand for football editing services has stronger evidence than demand for a standalone licensing product.** Marketplace order reviews go beyond stated interest. Affordable major-league footage and verified current small-creator losses remain unestablished in this batch.

| Question | What changed | Decision status |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer demand | Football-specific gig reviews display order-price bands; hiring requests ask for copyright-aware editing | Evidence of adjacent service demand; no verified demand for a new licensing product |
| Affordable licensing | Generic video starting prices and constraints inspected on supplier pages | No asset-level major-league offer plus applicable rights plus matching buyer budget verified |
| Current small-creator losses | One retrospective football account describes disruption and eventual payment | No new current, quantified, verified loss in the requested follower bands |

## Buyer demand

A football-editing Fiverr gig displays eight reviews. The inspected section includes four distinct reviewer handles and price bands ranging from up to $50 to $600–$800. This is **platform-displayed transaction evidence**, stronger than an unaccepted job advert, but not an audited invoice or independent proof of the deliverables. Repeated page rendering and one repeated reviewer are not additional buyers. Exact dates and channel sizes are unavailable. [D02: gig and review section](https://www.fiverr.com/faizan_rajput1/create-monetizable-and-copyright-free-football-videos-for-youtube-9be5).

A named hiring post for He’s A Baller requests recurring editing and sports copyright knowledge. It reports 5.2K YouTube subscribers alongside substantial reach elsewhere: a small channel count does not establish a small overall operator. A Twine personal-project request remains planning-stage intent. A Freelancer soccer request proposes revenue sharing rather than a reliable per-video budget; no promised revenue proof was inspected. [D03: hiring post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/harry-carter-2b6706235_videoeditor-youtube-hiring-activity-7272742494075863041-IDqk), [D04: personal project](https://www.twine.net/projects/b8yaq0-long-form-football-documentary-video-editing-video-editor-remote-job), [D05: partnership request](https://www.freelancer.com/projects/youtube/video-editor-partner-for-monetized).

**Interpretation:** people seek and apparently purchase football production help. Rights concerns can be part of that bundle. Neither the reviews nor adverts isolate a paid licensing need, prove lawful footage supply, or establish willingness to buy our proposed service. This is relevant buyer evidence for Gate 3, not Gate 3 approval.

## Licensing affordability

Pond5 advertises video starting at $39. Its governing agreement contains additional editorial, depicted-IP and audio restrictions; the presence of YouTube among digital distribution channels is not blanket permission for every asset or monetized use. No particular football match asset, its final price or applicable package was verified. [D09: pricing](https://www.pond5.com/pricing), [D10: agreement, particularly sections 3, 6, 7 and 9](https://www.pond5.com/legal/license).

AuthenticRights advertises a Creator plan starting at $79/month billed yearly. That is a general library offer: relevant competition footage, operative rights and eligibility were not established. Its advertised price therefore cannot be presented as affordable football licensing. [D08: pricing](https://authenticrights.com/pricing/).

British Pathé's original licensing page returned 403; its search snippet was not accepted. We did not submit an enquiry or circumvent the block.

**Affordability remains open.** A defensible finding requires a relevant asset and intended use, applicable rights, total cost including minimums/term, and a budget from the same buyer or comparable segment. Generic low starting prices and editing-order budgets do not supply that match. There is no conclusion that affordable footage does not exist.

## Current small-creator losses

A Reddit commenter describes a mixed real-football/FIFA channel at 20k subscribers, copyright-related interruption, eventual content reinstatement and receipt of the frozen money, followed by falling views and abandonment. The incident date is unspecified, the account is retrospective, and 20k lies outside the original 1,000–19,999 tiers. The comment does not establish current monetary loss or prove that enforcement caused the later traffic decline. [D11: original thread, Popular_Tomorrow_204 comment](https://www.reddit.com/r/SmallYoutubers/comments/1m06uxf/i_think_youtube_killed_my_channel/).

Two seemingly relevant leads were rejected: a recent income-interruption post does not establish a football channel; a football podcast's copyright discussion is opening music banter, not a reported loss. Channel revenue estimators, speculative monetization advice and sales copy were also excluded. The earlier small-creator reports remain valid as self-reports, with their original limitations; this batch does not convert them into verified financial evidence.

## What the research now supports

A bounded investigation of **copyright-aware football production services** is better supported than assuming a standalone footage-licensing product. This is a research interpretation, not a selected product or authorization to change scope. The numerical-data supplier study still cannot answer footage rights.

Keep all gate decisions pending. Before another broad search, specify whether the intended buyer is purchasing editing, sourcing permissioned footage, or resolving publication problems. The decision-changing evidence is then different: transaction scope and repeat demand; an asset-specific licence/price; or an attributable incident with dated actual financial consequences.

No outreach, purchase, quote request or merge was performed. The research is an English-language convenience sample, mainly YouTube-oriented, with stale/conflicting page dates and unverified identities. It cannot represent all regions or creator tiers.
