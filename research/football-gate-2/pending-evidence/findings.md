## An attributable production case

Cameron Fink describes removing desired footage from *Ange & The Boss* after a quoted $25,000 minimum and substituting Getty images. He also reports concessions from other providers. Treat the amount as his account of a quote, not a payment or FIFA's current price list; the currency is not explicitly stated. His post and transcript differ on clip length (17 versus 15 seconds). [G2P06: original post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/camfink_25000-for-17-seconds-thats-how-much-activity-7244460876974415873-HTZx).

Producer Tony Wilson's February 2024 post separately describes substantial archive-clearance costs for the same project. The project page names both filmmakers and later records screenings. These sources strengthen attribution and demonstrate an eventual route to release. They describe **one production**, and do not prove that all its production time was caused by licensing. The older account falls outside our preferred recency window. [G2P07: producer account](https://goodonewilson.substack.com/p/the-weight-of-the-archive-how-broadcasters), [G2P08: project record](https://documentaryaustralia.com.au/project/ange-and-the-boss-puskas-in-australia/).

This is evidence that rights costs can alter creative choices in a small documentary project. It is not proof that 1k–10k-follower social creators face the same economics, or that a new service can reduce the rights-holder's price.

## Additional experience and budget signals

A Reddit filmmaker describes months of work and unspecified expenditure on a historical World Cup documentary affected by Content ID/monetization claims. The film and enforcement notices were not supplied; claims, strikes and technical blocking must not be conflated. This adds a self-report of stakes, not verified financial loss (G2P01). [Original account](https://www.reddit.com/r/SportsDocumentaries/comments/1ug7gyn/has_anyone_successfully_resolved_a_fifa_content/).

A separate football-channel operator offers EUR10/day for ten simple image-based Shorts and reports making roughly fifteen in just over an hour. This is a posted outsourcing offer, not evidence that money changed hands or that the operator would buy footage licensing assistance. Its low-cost workflow also challenges assumptions of large editing burdens for every creator (G2P02). [Original offer and clarification](https://www.reddit.com/r/DoneDirtCheap/comments/1v9qcb6/for_hire_youtube_shorts_social_media_manager_for/).

## Permission arrangements: what is established

FIFA's public archive guide describes approved access and sales-reviewed collection orders. Its enquiry form asks for the project, usage, rights, territory, period and sublicensing needs. That documents a route to request permission; it supplies neither an automatic grant nor published affordable small-creator terms. No request was submitted. [G2P03: enquiry form](https://www.archives.fifa.com/new_project_enquiry_form), [G2P04: guide](https://www.archives.fifa.com/user_guide).

FIFA's March 2026 YouTube announcement distinguishes Media Partners from a creator cohort. It does not give every YouTube channel those rights. Bundesliga's August 2026 announcement documents specific UK/Ireland creator broadcasts and a major production partner. These disprove a blanket assertion that creators can never obtain access, while leaving ordinary small-channel eligibility and affordability unresolved. [G2P05: FIFA announcement](https://vod.fifa.com/organisation/media-releases/fifa-youtube-agreement-fifa-world-cup-2026-preferred-platform), [G2P09: Bundesliga announcement](https://www.bundesliga.com/en/bundesliga/news/bundesliga-media-expands-pioneering-uk-and-ireland-youtube-watch-along-coverage-with-jamie-vardy-partnership-goldbridge-2026-27-38838).

These World Cup and Bundesliga examples are limited adjacent feasibility checks. They do not establish coverage or rights across all six previously studied European competitions. Numerical-data permissions remain separate from footage permissions.

## Recommendation and stopping point

The narrow problem—licensing constraints altering or delaying football video production—is **supported for further investigation** across more than one source family. The evidence is still insufficient to approve a broad creator opportunity: the strongest attributable case is historical documentary production, recent loss reports remain unverified, and affordable equivalent access is unknown.

Stop generic searching here. The decision-changing evidence would be a current small creator's attributable project, itemized permission/acquisition burden and actual workflow outcome, paired with applicable supplier terms or a project-specific quote. A quote would test feasibility, not customer demand; actual purchasing evidence belongs to Gate 3. Further public research may locate such documentation, but cannot manufacture private invoices or contracts. No outreach has been conducted.
